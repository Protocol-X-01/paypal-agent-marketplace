## Shop image and availability handling — 9 November 2026

Product photos use more of the existing image area without changing card height or the two-column search grid; native thumbnails grow from 58 px to 64 px. Retailer rows marked out of stock are shown as non-clickable snapshots. Every retailer handoff checks the exact Channel3 product and selected merchant immediately before opening; product-level links may select another current in-stock retailer, while retailer-specific actions stop rather than silently switching. If the chosen retailer is no longer reported in stock, the recovery panel apologizes and immediately shows current offers for the same item plus up to three relevant alternatives Channel3 reports in stock. If no current stock is reported, the card uses the same recovery panel. Customers can also request alternatives when the retailer page says the item is sold out. Each choice gets its own exact-product and merchant recheck before opening. Similarity uses product title terms and a bounded category map; an unrecognized category may return no suggestion. These checks happen on demand; there is no periodic stock watcher or saved-item alert.

## Product boundaries

- Venmo remains the customer's wallet and payment surface. This concept does not connect to a Venmo account, hold keys, execute crypto trades, or complete retailer checkout.
- The current contact-payment flow only prepares a local Venmo draft for the customer to review and send themselves. PayPal's current [Venmo SDK v6 guide](https://developer.paypal.com/venmo/) documents merchant checkout for eligible US merchants and consumers in USD, with server-side order creation and capture. The separate [Payouts API](https://developer.paypal.com/payouts/overview/) handles business disbursements to PayPal or Venmo recipients. Venmo documents consumer peer-to-peer sending inside its own app ([send/request](https://help.venmo.com/cs/articles/sending-requesting-money-vhel171), [personal QR codes](https://help.venmo.com/cs/articles/personal-qr-codes-on-venmo-faq-vhel316)). These reviewed official flows do not establish an API for a third-party app to initiate a personal user's peer-to-peer payment. Keep the current contact flow draft-only; treat merchant checkout, business disbursements, AI-initiated purchases, and subscription management as distinct integrations with their own authorization, customer confirmation, durable order state, and idempotent event handling.
- Keep CoinGecko as the primary public market-data source for the broad dashboard: its [`/coins/markets` endpoint](https://docs.coingecko.com/reference/coins-markets) provides prices, market cap, volume and related fields, while [`/coins/{id}/market_chart`](https://docs.coingecko.com/reference/coins-id-market-chart) provides price, market-cap and volume series. Current evidence does not show that MetaMask provides more accurate coverage for the seven PayPal-listed assets; no matched-feed accuracy comparison has been run. MetaMask Agent Wallet exposes `mm price spot` and `mm price history` for CAIP-19 chain assets, and its [troubleshooting guide](https://docs.metamask.io/agent-wallet/troubleshooting/) notes that an asset may have no price. PayPal's [MetaMask provider listing](https://support.metamask.io/manage-crypto/move-crypto/buy/providers-and-payment-methods/) describes a U.S. buy-crypto on-ramp, not a market-data quality guarantee. Treat MetaMask as a possible wallet-specific supplementary quote after checking asset coverage, freshness and terms; keep market data separate from Venmo's wallet role.
- Manual portfolio values are estimates calculated in the browser from customer-entered quantities and public prices. They are not balances or financial advice.
- Channel3 searches and catalog loads are customer initiated. The shared catalog now has 14 verified category shelves and Target, CVS, Walmart and Best Buy retailer filters. A calendar-based seasonal edit brings four relevant shelves to the top and changes them quarterly; the remaining categories stay available, and current storefront offers and advertised commissions still load from Channel3 on selection. No prices, offers or commission rates are pinned. Curated browsing requires a valid server-side collection ID and cannot fall back to an unscoped browse; explicit text search may omit it, while a configured ID scopes search too. The new PayPal Markets — Everyday Discoveries collection was created on 2026-11-06 from the Twitch rules plus the four retailer inclusions; provider readback and runtime verification are separate acceptance checks. Customer-saved lists remain locally stored, with authenticated owner scopes where enabled; no cross-device sync is implemented. Retailer handoff refreshes the exact product first.
- The repository now contains a responsive website and a separate Expo Android/iOS companion. Selected screens and interactions have been reviewed on a Xiaomi debug build; full physical-device acceptance and release remain open.
- Dark visual direction (user confirmed 2026-11-08): follow PayPal's supplied dark-theme references with black/charcoal surfaces, white/gray supporting copy and selective blue/cyan accents. PayPal+ is a visual reference, not an active feature requirement. The native Rewards tab, screen and Shop card are removed from customer flows. The website guide remains parked in `src/integrations/paypal-plus/`, outside active navigation. No points, tiers or PayPal+ activity are read or redeemed. Channel3 advertiser commission remains separate from customer rewards. PayPal's [UK help page](https://www.paypal.com/uk/cshelp/article/what-is-paypal-help1286) and [terms](https://www.paypal.com/uk/legalhub/paypal/paypalplus-tnc?locale.x=en_GB) describe PayPal+ as a personal UK account programme. A review of PayPal's [public API catalogue](https://developer.paypal.com/api/rest/current-resources/) on 2026-11-08 found no documented customer points/balance resource; any future connection remains gated on PayPal-confirmed API access, scopes and consent. Do not screen-scrape PayPal or infer live points from an Activity export.
- PayPal app continuity from the supplied UI references: Markets serves as Home; the existing draft/contact workspace is the safe Send foundation; Shop carries Channel3 discovery; Account serves as Me/Personal Lite and Business. PayPal+ screenshots guide the visual theme only; do not copy merchant campaigns, tier-linked product earn rates or Pay Later promotions into Channel3 without a verified campaign feed and product-level eligibility source. Pool/Split/other actual transfers remain future PayPal/Venmo capabilities, not actions in this concept.
- The native customer app now has a dedicated Send tab with validated USD amount entry and suggested amounts. Continuing transfers the amount through a five-minute in-memory intent to the existing Contacts & draft section; the amount itself is not placed in the route URL or persisted by Send, and its input and pending intent clear when leaving the tab or backgrounding the app. Portfolio asks before replacing a different existing draft amount. It does not send money, initiate a request, or open Venmo. Android/iOS and tablet acceptance remain pending a connected-device review.

### 2. Identity, payments, and security

- Define an authenticated identity and ownership model before syncing contacts or portfolios across devices, or storing payment preferences and orders on the server. Manually entered Venmo contacts remain device-local.
- Preserve customer confirmation and provider-owned checkout. Any future payment implementation needs persisted order state, ownership checks, webhook replay protection, and idempotency.
- The local Channel3, CoinGecko, and PayPal server requests reject redirects before forwarding credentials or webhook bodies. Synthetic local HTTPS tests cover ordinary responses and same- and cross-origin redirects. These redirect guards are included in released commit `ec25028c7651c59c199a5d58f53daf302a20d652` (verified against the isolated release checkout); no redirect or exposure by a real provider was established. A provider that requires a redirect will be unavailable until its endpoint is reviewed.
- The PayPal webhook validates required headers and bounds body size and verification time; it limits body-read/signature-verification work to eight concurrent requests per Node process and returns `503` with `Retry-After` when full. PayPal retries non-2xx deliveries up to 25 times over three days ([delivery behavior](https://developer.paypal.com/api/rest/webhooks/rest/)). The per-process cap is not shared across instances. The endpoint acknowledges valid deliveries without processing payments. Add shared capacity controls, replay protection, and idempotency before processing any payment event.
- A [local synthetic webhook simulation](../postman/simulations/paypal-webhook-verification.md) covers successful and rejected verification, malformed requests, provider failure, and timeout using the receiver and adapter behind a small HTTP bridge. It does not exercise the Next.js runtime or a signed PayPal sandbox delivery.
- Baseline response security headers are set in `next.config.ts`. A limited Content Security Policy restricts form destinations and base URLs to the app's origin, disallows object embeds, and prevents this app from being framed. It does not restrict scripts, images, network connections or child frames, and is not a complete XSS defense. Browser acceptance is still open. Any future direct PayPal form submission or intentional framing requires reviewing these directives and the existing `X-Frame-Options` header. Define and test a stricter script/style policy after documenting the required origins and nonce/rendering tradeoffs. The Channel3 proxy rejects JSON bodies above 8 KiB or slower than three seconds and admits at most eight concurrent body readers per process, then keys its rate limiter on Render's Cloudflare-verified `CF-Connecting-IP`; when that header is missing or invalid, it uses one shared unknown-address bucket. In-memory limits and the PayPal verification concurrency cap are per process; use shared storage or a durable queue before scaling across instances, and enforce connection limits at the edge. Do not use the first `X-Forwarded-For` value because caller-supplied entries can be preserved by the proxy chain.
- Add a focused threat model and operational review for each enabled integration and any new customer data flow.
- On 2026-11-06, a private compatibility adapter was added locally so Expo Router's CommonJS dependency uses the patched upstream URI decoder without changing SDK 57. The production dependency audit reports four moderate findings and no high findings; the remaining entries involve Expo fingerprint/build tooling. Synthetic decoder and Router tests passed, but malformed input can decode differently from the former version. The configured routing path uses `URLSearchParams`, and reachability of the old vulnerable fallback was not established. Verify Android and iOS runtime behavior before release, and remove the adapter when a compatible upstream fix is available. [Decoder advisory](https://github.com/advisories/GHSA-qr28-fpjm-wq7f), [Expo tracking issue](https://github.com/expo/expo/issues/49943)
- Retained synthetic Android and iOS decoder/Router probes also passed in a standalone host Hermes V1 VM built from the matching official version, and a deliberate failing assertion exited nonzero. The installed APK library matched the cached Hermes artifact. These checks cover the probe's decoder and module interop; they do not execute the native app or resolve its grey screen.

Review the applicable regional agreement and retention requirements before keeping historical customer-health or retention metrics; do not assume derived aggregates are exempt.
- Use approved company data with purpose and access checks. Do not show row-level customer PII by default or present sample metrics as live data.
- Elastic app telemetry can show this product's health; it does not provide PayPal customer insights by itself.
- Establish data source, access roles, retention, redaction, and alert ownership before sending customer or request data to monitoring services.
